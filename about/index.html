About

I am Albert Arnó, MD, PhD — a physician working in health technology from Barcelona, Spain.

In 2000 I founded onmedic, a studio that builds software for hospitals, research centres, laboratories and medical practices. Most of that work is confidential and always will be: clinical data systems, research software, the infrastructure that sits underneath other people's names. It taught me the thing that still governs how I build — that a health record is not a preference, and that the safest place for one is the smallest number of places.

Most of my own building lately has gone into AnalyticsZone, a family of sports and health analytics apps. They read what your watch already recorded and compute the models a coach would actually use: critical power and W′ balance, critical swim speed, gait asymmetry, heart-rate variability, training load. Every model is a published one, cited on the page that explains it, and all of it is computed on the device rather than on a server I control.

Alongside that there is Pelvisana for pelvic-floor education, Velytra for coaches and their athletes, and DoctorMi for the unglamorous problem of non-urgent clinical messaging.

What I will and will not claim

I am a doctor, and the software I write touches health data, so it matters that the line is explicit. Nothing I publish here or ship in an app is a diagnosis, and none of it replaces a clinical assessment. What these tools do is compute defined metrics from sensor data and explain, with citations, what the literature says those metrics mean — including where they stop being reliable. Where I do not know something, the honest version is on the page.
